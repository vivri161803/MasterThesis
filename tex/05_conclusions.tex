\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\section{Objectives and how they were met}
\label{sec:conclusions-summary}

This thesis set out to document two systems built at Dometria AI against the two problems stated in \S\ref{sec:intro-problems}, and to state precisely what their evaluation establishes.

Against the first problem — turning a heterogeneous document into a structured, comparable asset record — \texttt{dometria-bulk-insert} was built and is described in \S\ref{sec:bulk-insert}. It classifies a document to one of 97 contract types through a constrained LLM call followed by weighted embedding similarity, generates the corresponding Pydantic schema at runtime from a field tree unioned offline over those contracts, and extracts field values in per-topic groups with self-consistency voting over the mandatory ones. Trustworthiness, identified in \S\ref{sec:intro-problems} as the actual difficulty, is addressed structurally rather than by post-hoc checking: every populated field carries a verbatim quote and is rejected without one, every closed vocabulary offers an explicit abstention value, and arithmetic and cross-field values are computed in Python from transcribed primitives rather than requested from the model.

Against the second problem — matching an asset that has no interaction history — ColdRAG was adapted to the real-estate off-market setting and is described in \S\ref{sec:coldrag-system}. Its ingestion pipeline, derived from DIAL-KG \citep{bao2026dial}, builds a knowledge graph in Neo4j through deterministic product-node creation, LLM entity extraction, vector-plus-judge named entity disambiguation, and a governance layer that rejects a proposed fact only when the source text contradicts it. Retrieval proceeds by multi-hop traversal with LLM edge scoring and threshold pruning, ranks candidates by blind match, and perturbs numeric metadata under $\varepsilon$-differential privacy before exposure. Demand-side input is acquired through a human-in-the-loop conversation validated turn by turn against a Pydantic model generated at runtime from the taxonomy.

Both systems run, are exercised by a test suite on every commit, and carry instrumentation built for the failure modes their own architecture admits. What was not established is accuracy. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports evaluation instruments and illustrative figures, and reports no measured accuracy benchmark for either system, because neither system's documentation contains one and this thesis did not construct one post hoc (\S\ref{sec:results-limitations}). The objective of \S\ref{sec:intro-contributions} was met in the sense of design, implementation, and honest characterization; it was not met in the sense of demonstrated output quality, and \S\ref{sec:conclusions-limitations} treats that distinction as the central limitation of the work.

\section{Cross-project synthesis}
\label{sec:conclusions-synthesis}

The two systems address opposite ends of one data pipeline. \texttt{dometria-bulk-insert} operates on the supply side, converting the documents in which asset information arrives into records with a known schema; ColdRAG operates on the demand side, acquiring a counterparty's requirements conversationally and matching them against those records. Neither is useful alone in the setting of \S\ref{sec:intro-context}: matching has nothing to match on without structured records, and structured records serve no purpose in an off-market context that forbids publishing them.

They also share a method, and the sharing is more specific than a common stack. \emph{Transcribe-then-derive} (\S\ref{sec:evidence-grounded-principle}) appears in both at three different granularities. At field granularity, an extracted value without a supporting quote is rejected before the record is assembled (\S\ref{sec:bulk-insert-block3}). At edge granularity, a proposed graph relation is adjudicated against the text that produced it before it is written (\S\ref{sec:coldrag-ingestion}). At conversational-turn granularity, a payload is emitted only once every mandatory field has been extracted from something the user actually said and validated against the generated model (\S\ref{sec:coldrag-hitl}). In each case the LLM is asked to transcribe and the system decides, rather than the reverse.

A second commonality follows from the first. In both systems the schema is data rather than code: the extraction schema is derived from a field tree and a metadata sidecar compiled offline, the conversational schema from \texttt{rules.yaml}, and both are instantiated as Pydantic models at runtime. This is what makes the evidence gate enforceable uniformly — validation is ordinary Python running against a declared model (\S\ref{sec:structured-output}) — and it is also what makes retargeting a configuration change in principle rather than a rewrite.

The consequence for evaluation was drawn in \S\ref{sec:results-synthesis} and is worth restating as a conclusion in its own right. Both systems place verification inside the pipeline rather than after it, which is what makes their output defensible by construction: an unsupported value does not reach the record or the graph to begin with. The same choice is why no held-out set exists on which an external harness could be run. The architecture that makes these systems trustworthy in the small is the architecture that leaves them unmeasured in the large.

One qualification belongs here rather than in the limitations. The pipeline described above is architectural, not yet wired: the two systems were built as separate services sharing a stack and a method, and ColdRAG currently ingests documents directly rather than consuming the records \texttt{dometria-bulk-insert} produces (\S\ref{sec:coldrag-ingestion}). Joining them is treated in \S\ref{sec:conclusions-future-work}.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusions-limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No accuracy benchmark.} Neither system has been evaluated against a held-out labeled reference — field-level accuracy for extraction, a ranking metric for matching (\S\ref{sec:results-limitations}). Nor has match quality been assessed by domain experts, which for a matching system is the judgment that ultimately matters. The instrumentation that does exist measures self-consistency, vocabulary coverage, latency, and cost, and none of these is a proxy for correctness.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility is bounded, not achieved.} Every LLM call is issued at temperature 0 with an optional fixed seed, but identical prompts are not guaranteed to return identical output \citep{he2025defeating}. Self-consistency voting absorbs part of the residue and \texttt{measure\_variance.py} measures what remains (\S\ref{sec:results-bulk-insert-tooling}); neither removes it. ColdRAG has no equivalent variance instrument, so run-to-run stability of its rankings is uncharacterized.
  \item \textbf{Cost and latency scale with the number of LLM calls.} Extraction issues one call per topic group and more where voting applies; retrieval issues a query analysis, one edge-scoring pass per hop, and a final ranking pass, so a query's cost grows with how deep the multi-hop loop goes (\S\ref{sec:coldrag-inference}). The figures recorded in \S\ref{sec:results-coldrag-run} are illustrative of a single run and are not a cost model.
  \item \textbf{Dependence on external model providers.} Both systems reach their models through a third-party gateway (\S\ref{sec:shared-stack}). This decouples the systems from any one vendor, but it also means model behaviour can change under a configuration that has not changed, which the current instrumentation would surface only as drift in downstream output.
  \item \textbf{Scope is one domain and one language.} The field tree derives from 97 Italian real-estate contract types and ColdRAG's taxonomy from a single \texttt{rules.yaml}. Domain-independence is a property of the architecture as described in \S\ref{sec:conclusions-synthesis}, not a demonstrated result: neither system has been retargeted to a second domain, so the claim remains untested.
  \item \textbf{The privacy guarantee is per-exposure.} The Laplace mechanism is applied to numeric metadata at the point of exposure at a fixed budget (\S\ref{sec:coldrag-privacy}). No accounting of that budget across successive queries or sessions is described in the system's specification, so the composition guarantee over a sequence of queries — which is the regime the re-identification risk of \S\ref{sec:blind-matching} actually arises in — is not established.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conclusions-future-work}

\textbf{A formal benchmark against baselines.} The first limitation above is also the first priority, and the hard part is the reference set rather than the metric. Extraction requires documents annotated field by field; matching requires relevance judgments over asset-requirement pairs, against which Recall@$k$ or NDCG (\S\ref{sec:cf-content-hybrid}) and the reference-free RAG evaluation frameworks surveyed in \S\ref{sec:rag} could be applied \citep{es2024ragas,chen2024benchmarking}. Beyond an absolute figure, the measurements worth having are ablations of the design decisions this thesis argues for: multi-hop traversal against flat vector retrieval, to isolate what the graph contributes; and extraction with the evidence gate against extraction without it, to quantify what the gate costs in recall for what it buys in precision.

\textbf{Dynamic ingestion from a structured store.} Wiring ColdRAG's ingestion to consume the records \texttt{dometria-bulk-insert} emits — or a relational database holding them — would close the pipeline of \S\ref{sec:conclusions-synthesis}. The present separation means a document routed through both systems is extracted twice by two independent LLM pipelines, with no guarantee the two agree; consuming the audited record instead would make the graph's attributes exactly the ones that passed the evidence gate, and would remove one full extraction pass from the cost of onboarding an asset.

\textbf{Product lifecycle and deprecation.} Ingestion writes through \texttt{MERGE} and nothing described retires what it has written (\S\ref{sec:coldrag-ingestion}), so an asset that has been sold or withdrawn remains retrievable. Handling this requires more than deletion: entities shared with still-active products must survive their neighbour's removal, so the graph needs an explicit status on the product node, filtering at retrieval time, and a policy for entities left orphaned — a lifecycle interacting directly with the disambiguation logic that caused the sharing in the first place.

\textbf{Collaborative signal in the graph.} Cold start is a starting condition, not a permanent one. As the system is used, interactions accumulate, and knowledge-graph recommenders already propagate user preference over exactly this kind of attribute graph \citep{wang2019kgat,guo2022survey}, so recorded interactions could enter as edges rather than as a separate model — moving the system toward the hybrid of \S\ref{sec:cf-content-hybrid} \citep{burke2002hybrid} without abandoning the content-only path that must remain available for every newly ingested asset. This also supplies what the first item lacks: a confirmed match is a relevance judgment, and enough of them constitute the reference set against which the system could finally be measured.
